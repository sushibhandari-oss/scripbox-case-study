Performance reporting

Every month, a Relationship Manager (RM) would get on a call with the assisted clients and help them understand their portfolio through a PDF performance report.

The portfolio report contains

  1. Overall wealth A single view of everything the client owned across mutual funds, PMS, bonds, and stocks.
  2. Long-term growth How the portfolio's value moved over eight years.
  3. Downside protection How the portfolio held up against the index during a market downturn.
  4. Benchmarking How the equity portfolio performed relative to the index.
  5. Rebalancing A recommended set of changes to reduce fund overlap and simplify the portfolio.
+ and many more

But none of it lived on the Client Portal,
which led to:

Problem 1
Gated by an RM

No access without an RM. Performance data lived with someone else, to see it, you had to ask.

Problem 2
Static reporting

Updated once a month. Whatever changed in between was invisible until the next report.

Problem 3
No room to explore

Just a page. Nothing to click, filter, or dig into.

The PDF report had nearly 20 types of reporting. What should make it to the Client Portal?

Redesigning the invest flow

Opening the portal to everyone meant closing a gap first: transactions.

Assisted clients never needed to transact themselves. Their RM did it for them. But a portal for everyone meant the portal itself had to handle it.

Client portal checklist

Investing
Withdrawing

75%+ of users dropped off at the first step of the old invest flow.

It couldn't be ported over as it was. It needed multiple UX improvements first.

  1. Too many decisions in a single step Investment type, amount, schedule, step-up, and fund selection were all packed into a single screen, increasing cognitive load.
  2. Primary CTA below the fold Users had to scroll before reaching the primary action, delaying progression through the flow.
  3. Hidden fund customization The option to customize recommended funds was buried below the fold, making it easy to miss.
  4. No sense of progression The journey lacked a clear structure, making it difficult for users to understand where they were and what came next.
  5. Weak visual hierarchy Primary actions, secondary actions, and supporting information competed for attention instead of guiding users through the experience.

We redesigned the invest flow around four key principles.

  1. One decision at a time The investment journey was broken into four focused steps, allowing users to make one decision at a time instead of navigating a long, overwhelming form.
  2. Clear sense of progress A progress indicator provided visibility into where users were in the journey and what remained, helping set expectations throughout the flow.
  3. Consistent primary actions A sticky primary CTA remained visible across every step, while page-level and in-step actions were clearly differentiated to create predictable interaction patterns.
  4. Stronger information hierarchy Primary tasks were prioritized, while supporting information like summaries and projections was positioned contextually, helping users focus on the right decision at the right time.
