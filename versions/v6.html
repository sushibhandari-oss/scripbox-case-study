Chapter 02 · The report

04 · The report

The thing assisted clients valued most was a monthly PDF.

The Monthly Portfolio Review: monthly and annual change, benchmarking against the index and inflation, cash flows, rebalancing suggestions, exit recommendations. Genuinely useful data. Also static, already dated on arrival, and only as fresh as the last time your RM hit send.

Sent 43 days ago
05 · The report

A report this useful shouldn't arrive once a month.

Everything in the Monthly Portfolio Review described a moving portfolio, frozen at one date. Bringing it into the portal meant numbers that are current when you log in, benchmarks you can actually explore, and recommendations you can act on while they're still relevant. It also gave assisted clients a reason to open the portal at all.

06 · The report

So we rebuilt the report as the heart of the portal.

Live performance, benchmarking, cash flows, and recommendations, restructured into the portal's information architecture. A structure built for occasional viewing doesn't hold up once reporting becomes the main reason to visit, so the IA changed with it.

07 · The report

The portal built for 6,000 clients turned out better than the website serving ten times that.

This wasn't the plan. But when Scripbox compared the two, the client portal communicated more, and more clearly, than the self-serve website their roughly 60,000 digital users were on.

Self-serve web · ~60,000 users
Client portal · ~6,000 clients
08 · The report

So Scripbox decided to stop maintaining two websites.

The portal would become the platform for all users, and the legacy Phoenix codebase would be shut down. The product we'd built for one segment became the foundation of their entire web experience.
